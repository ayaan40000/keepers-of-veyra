# 1. The Great Forgetting, reworked: the Seam and the Winder

Draft for the owner, Nov 2, 2026. Nothing here is wired into the game. The matching code-shaped draft is `forgetting.js` in this folder.

## The idea in one paragraph

Under the Undercroft, below the Last Stone, is the place where the Lethe truly rises. Three hundred years ago a Warden stepped into it and snagged time on a thread, and he has been holding that thread ever since. The Tide that rose last year was higher than any in three hundred years because he pulled harder. What the city called forgetting was partly something else: days caught on the thread and pulled out of order. The Great Forgetting is those days, gathered into a giant of mist. Remembering them in their true order stitches the rift shut, and the Warden, who only ever wanted not to be forgotten, is remembered and lets go.

## The rift: "the Seam"

Name for players: **the Seam** (a place where two edges of time were sewn badly and have been pulling at each other). Veyrans will say "the Tangle" in conversation. The Great Forgetting is its shape.

### Rules of the Seam

1. **It lies where the river rises.** It is the warm place at the bottom of the Undercroft, where the Last Stone tablet says the Lethe "is not cold, it is warm, like breath". The Tide is the river breathing out. The Seam is a knot in the river's keeping.
2. **It takes nothing away.** The river keeps every day it takes (the Undercroft says so). At the Seam, days were pulled up the wrong way on a thread. They are tangled, not destroyed.
3. **Tangled feels different from forgotten.** A forgotten thing is simply gone and the person is calm about it. A tangled thing is known but in the wrong place: a baker with loaves for the wrong day, a woman with three names for one dog, two old men who each remember the other in a different year. All three are in the shipped Prologue, so the first scene of the game already shows the Seam without saying so.
4. **It cannot be closed by force, by wishing, or by forgetting.** More forgetting feeds it (this is why Sevrin's Great Mercy would have been a disaster). Only remembering things in their true order closes it. That is what "stitching" means.
5. **When it closes, days go home in time.** Every snagged day returns to its place in the person it came from.
6. **It does not undo what was chosen or what the river takes in the ordinary way.** Tomas Hask's grey, Sevrin's old grief, Wren's mother's face (lost to an ordinary Tide when Wren was eight): all stay as they are. The Tide still rises thin every evening afterwards. Ordinary forgetting stays normal and forgivable.
7. **Time is thin inside it.** A person in the Seam does not age the way others do. That is how the Winder is still alive.

## The antagonist: Ambrose Tallow, called "the Winder"

- **Who.** The last Warden of the Hours, the Warden who kept the Tide's timetable and wound the Mother Clock. Three hundred years ago, as the lamps went out in the Long Quiet, he went down to the bottom of the stair to find out why and stepped into the Seam to hold the last evening in place. A thin, tidy man in an old Warden's coat with the brass badge worn smooth. A pocket watch on a long chain. Kind, tired eyes. His mark is a ring with a single hand, like a clock's.
- **What he says he wants.** To keep the city's days safe, every one in its place, so that nothing is ever lost.
- **What he really wants (the ulterior motive).** To be remembered. The Seam cost him his name: nobody has said it in three hundred years, and his line in the Heartledger's roll of Wardens has faded to a smudge. He wanted the Heartledger (the Hushed's "Heart") to read his own name again, and to re-order Veyra's days around his one perfect evening, in which everyone knows him. He tells himself it is for the city's sake. He is lonely, and he has begun to tell himself that the city's days belong to him.
- **How he relates to the other villain.** He never met Sevrin. He paid for the Order's oil and wicks for eleven years through a purse with his mark, and let Sevrin believe the Great Mercy was the Order's own idea. A mass forgetting would have pulled every day in Veyra onto his thread.
- **Why he is gentle.** He is not cruel, and nothing in the game is frightening. He is the same wound as Sevrin's, turned inside out: Sevrin wanted a city that forgot everything, the Winder wants a city that forgets nothing. Calder's lesson (choose what to keep) answers both.
- **Hidden in plain sight.** His name is in the third verse of the "Lamplighter's Song", which Wren's mother hummed as "The Last Lamp". The verse says "Tallow keeps the lamp for me". Everyone has taken "Tallow" to be candle fat. It is his name.

### The third verse (new, written for this proposal)

> Trim the wick and turn the key,
> Tallow keeps the lamp for me.
> By river stones and light, go home:
> the last lamp waits, and none walk alone.

The shipped song has two verses and the Chronolog entry `song` says Tansy sings it. The third verse is "the part the Quay folk only hum". Tansy hums it in the shipped Epilogue ("something with no name that she seemed to know all the same"). That is the Last Lamp.

## Chapter 13: The Seam (proposed)

**Where.** After the Year of Keeping is complete (the existing unlock). It is the true last chapter. The shipped Epilogue remains the gentle summing-up before it, and the Great Forgetting becomes the reason the Tide is climbing in the Year of Keeping. The Great Forgetting keeps its shipped picture (a hooded giant of grey mist with a pale glow for a face), its id `great_forgetting`, its gate `M12` and its coach (Wren).

**What the giant is.** The days snagged on the Seam, gathered into a body. It is not the Winder. He is inside the glow, holding it up like a lantern. In the old draft the giant was "every lost thing gathered". Now it is every tangled day gathered, and the small Forgettings (Smudges, Tangles, Hollows and the rest) ride in it, because tangled days and ordinary lost things drift together in the same mist.

**The battle.** Same Echo rules as every battle: made-up material, no Lumen, no relics, no skill changes, answers shown after a miss, rest allowed at any time. Four phases.

1. **The Hem of the Cloak** (Visual Association, Story Linking, Memory Palace). Unchanged from the first draft except for a rift line: the small shapes at the hem are days that are out of order, not lost.
2. **The Body of Mist** (Names and Faces, Number Encoding). Unchanged except for the same framing: faces and dates, waiting to be put back in order.
3. **The Pale Glow** (Vocabulary and Concepts, Structured Knowledge, then all seven mixed). The last small shapes go home. The glow turns and the Winder is seen. He talks to Wren. He offers to keep the year for the player, forever, if he may have the Heartledger. Wren answers him. He says, mildly, "Then show me," and sets the Stitch.
4. **The Stitch** (a recall task, built from all seven skills). At the start of the battle the player studies a thread of nine beads, nine made-up days of the city (a copper kettle rolling down Lantern Street, a heron on the ferry rope, a wedding of nine green umbrellas, and so on). Three phases of other questions pass. Then the Stitch asks for the nine in true order. That is a real delayed ordered recall, built from tools the player already has (picture, link, place). Closing the Seam needs six of nine in place. Missing is fine: the answer is shown and the Stitch is simply not tied off yet. The player can rest and try again tomorrow with no penalty. The result does not touch skill estimates.

**Final beat.** The thread is tied. The Winder is thin as glass. He says he cannot remember what to be called. Wren hums "The Last Lamp". One story-memory question, ungraded: "Whose name is in the third verse?" If the player kept it from the Wren arc, they answer "Tallow". If not, Wren says it for them, warmly. The player opens the Heartledger's roll of Wardens in their mind and the smudged last line clears: **Ambrose Tallow, Warden of the Hours, who kept the last lamp.** He lets go of the thread. The Seam closes "as quietly as a door in another house".

## The new ending and epilogue ("Days in Their Places")

Shown once after the first victory. Plain, warm, short beats:

1. **The clocks.** All 112 clocks in the Market click from nine minutes past eleven to ten past, and start telling time again, on the right day.
2. **The Prologue's people.** The baker remembers the loaves were for a christening on the Tidal Stair and runs. The woman's dog turns out to have three names, given by three children in three different years, and answers to all of them. The two old men are brothers, and each is right about a different year.
3. **Mosk's wife's name** (taken by the Seam, not the Tide) comes back: Wenna. He says it twice, then goes to sit by his boat.
4. **The ledgers.** The old ink comes back faintly under Wren's new entries, like a watermark. Wren decides to leave her pages as they are. The city wrote them again in its own words, and she likes those.
5. **Wren and her mother.** The face does not come back (the river took it long ago, in the ordinary way). Wren's father, Edrin, describes it, and the details the player has been carrying all year (see document 2) become a portrait Wren paints. It is near enough to say good night to. She says good night to the last lamp, and it brightens.
6. **Calder's explanation.** Calder (as Tansy) says what she never said: the river was already at the sluice, and a hand's width kept the Gate standing. She takes back nothing, except the words of a song.
7. **The Winder.** He sits by the last lamp with a cup of rosemary tea. Tansy says he smells of lamp oil. He winds the Mother Clock once, then goes to sit by the water. The Quay folk leave a lamp lit for him.
8. **The Tide comes back the next night**, thin and silver, and leaves four or five small shapes, no larger than lint. The shipped `BOSS_EPILOGUE` is kept as the last beat.

## What stays exactly as shipped

- The Tide is the river being the river.
- Ordinary forgetting is normal and forgivable.
- Forgettings are lost things waiting to be remembered, and are never driven off.
- The Great Forgetting is not destroyed. It is remembered and goes home.
- Sevrin's story, Calder's choice, Tomas Hask's state, and Wren's mother's face.
