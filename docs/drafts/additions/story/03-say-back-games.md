# 3. The Say-Back Games: Veyra's own memory contests, and the rival

Draft for the owner, Nov 2, 2026. This is the story wrapper for the Event Hall in `docs/CURRICULUM_ADDITIONS.md` (sections 5 and 11). No real championship is named. Nothing is wired.

## Why Veyra competes (the backstory)

**The Long Vigil made the Games.** In the first years after the Compact, the whole city stayed awake on the Night of No Moon with lamps in their hands, holding the Gate. It was a very long night and people kept nodding off. So neighbours began to test each other in the dark: "Tell me the names on my street." "Say back the verse we sang an hour ago." "What was in the third row of the market?" Whoever could say it back kept the others awake and the lamps lit. That is the whole origin: **the Say-Back**. Saying a thing back to someone is a promise that you will remember them.

**The Wardens made it a ladder.** Ysolde and her twelve noticed that the best Say-Back players were the ones who could carry a route through a thin Tide. So the Wardens turned it into a way to find new Wardens: any citizen could enter, the Quay Heat was open to everyone, and the best climbed through the districts until the last round was held in the Amphitheatre before the Warden's own lamp. That is the link to the Art of Places. Each event was a Warden's duty in play form (see the table below).

**The Rite is the hard old cousin.** The shipped Rite of Echoes is the grim form, where an office is at stake and the loser drinks the grey. It has not been called in two hundred years because, after the last one, the city agreed that nobody should lose a name for losing a game. The Games are the kind form: the only thing you can lose is a ribbon. Sevrin's challenge in Chapter 11 revived the cruel form on purpose. The Games are what the city goes back to afterwards.

**The Long Quiet shrank them.** When paper became cheaper than practice, the Games became a hobby of the rich: entry fees, private tutors, a Cup kept in a glass case. They survived, barely, because one family kept them going, and kept them for themselves. (That family is the rival's.)

**Why the people still love it.** Winners' names are read aloud at the Vigil every year. To be said back by the whole city once a year is the highest honour in Veyra. Ordinary players enter because a Say-Back is how neighbours say "I will remember you".

**The Cup.** The prize is the **Lamp Cup**, a small brass lamp on a stand. Its founder's plate is worn blank. Nobody remembers who first set the Say-Back in order. The Gilt family have polished that plate for generations without ever reading it. (It was Ambrose Tallow, the Winder. The plate is restored in the ending: see document 1.)

## The tiers

Season name: **The Say-Back Games**. A season has five tiers. Each tier raises the scale of the events (more faces, longer rows, longer verses) and adds a new event. The Event Hall's saved best results are the player's personal records and are what the rival is set against.

| Tier | Name | Where | New event | Play this many of the available events | Scale (example) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **The Quay Heat** | Lamplighters' Quay, at lamp-lighting | Hall Round, Vigil Verse, Rook Run | 3 of 3 | 12 faces, 4 lines, 10 cards |
| 2 | **The Gallery Heat** | The Long Gallery | Counting Rows, Bridge Tally | 3 of 5 | 16 faces, 1 row, 20 words |
| 3 | **The City Cup** | The Harbour, on the quay steps | Three Lamps Out | 4 of 6 | 20 faces, 2 rows, 8 lines |
| 4 | **The Compact Round** | The round hall under the copper dome | Double Rook | 5 of 7 | 24 faces, 3 rows, 12 lines |
| 5 | **The Lamp Final** | The Amphitheatre of Echoes | none | all 7, from your best marks | full scale |

Rules the owner asked for:

- **Tiers rise to a final.** The next tier opens when the player has posted results in the required number of events of the tier before. "Posted a result" means played, not passed. There is no passing mark, so a hard day does not block anyone.
- **The Final is a night, not a grind.** The player's best result in each of the seven events is used. They may replay any event to improve it first. Then the Cup night plays out as a short story scene: seven lamps are lit one at a time, with the player's mark and the rival's mark side by side. The player wins by lighting four of the seven (their mark at least the rival's). Ties go to the player.
- **When it opens.** Recommendation: the Event Hall (practice and best results) opens with the Amphitheatre district after the M10 gate, as already decided. The Season (tiers and rival) begins after the M12 gate, as the city's celebration after the Vigil, which is also when the Games story makes sense. The Season has no deadline. It can be played alongside the Year of Keeping and does not need to be finished before the Great Forgetting.

## The rival's scores: scripted, calm, and against the player's own best

The rule in plain words. The rival never races the player and never watches a timer. The rival is a story that happens after each tier, not during it. When a tier opens, the game takes a snapshot of the player's best result in each event. The rival's mark for the tier is that snapshot multiplied by a factor that falls tier by tier. The player never sees the rival's mark before they have posted their own. After the tier, a scene shows both marks and the rival speaks.

| Tier | Rival's mark is the player's snapshot times | One scripted stumble | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1.10 | none | She starts ahead. She has tutors and prepared pictures. |
| 2 | 1.07 | none | Her prepared pictures stop covering the bigger scale. |
| 3 | 1.04 | Three Lamps Out: 0.85 | Her first stumble. |
| 4 | 1.01 | none | The tutors are gone. It is close. |
| Final | 1.04 on Hall Round, Vigil Verse, Rook Run, Counting Rows; 0.97 on Bridge Tally, Three Lamps Out, Double Rook | none | Four events she should win, three she should lose. The snapshot is taken when the Final opens, so a player who replays and improves even one event wins. One who does not is a single improvement short. |

Guards:

- **Capped at 96 percent** of the event's maximum, so she never posts a perfect score and the player is never chasing an impossible one.
- **No best yet?** A default table is used (a modest mark for that event and scale), so nobody is penalised for skipping an event.
- **Never a pressure.** No countdown to the next rival score, no leaderboard, no "she is catching up" nag. The player sees her progress only in the post-tier scene and in a small "Ottoline's marks" board in the Event Hall.
- **Never touches skills.** Event results do not change skill estimates, Lumen, or anything else (the same rule as the Crypt and trials).
- **Rewards from learning only.** The Event Hall rewards are best-result ladders only, as decided. Beating the rival gives the Cup scene and a Chronolog entry, nothing else. Because her marks are set against the player's own earlier best, the only way to beat them is to have actually improved, which is the point.
- **Never unwinnable, never a lock.** A player who loses the Final can replay any events and see the Cup night again whenever they like, with the same rival marks.
- **Honest wording.** "Scores measure performance on Mnemos tasks."

The exact factors are proposals. A small simulation like `scripts/crypt-sim.mjs` should check that a typical player wins about two Finals in three, and that nobody who improves by a little can fail to win.

## The rival: Ottoline Gilt

- **Who.** Nineteen. Only daughter of Gilt & Daughters, lamp-oil and wicks, the richest supplier in Veyra. Tall, immaculate, a ribbon in her hair for each tier she has won. She has not walked a route on her own in her life. Her family has seven tutors, one for each event, and a very small man whose only job is to carry the first tutor's umbrella.
- **How the tutors help.** They prepare her pictures for her. She is fast on a prepared picture and brittle on a new one. She believes this is the same thing as skill.
- **Voice.** Smug, bratty, quick. "Honestly." "I am told." "Do try to keep up." Plenty of barbs, none cruel: she mocks the player's old lantern, their "homemade" pictures, their lack of tutors, never their body, their family or their worth. She calls the player **Lantern** at first, and **{name}** by the end.
- **What she is hiding.** She is frightened of being nothing without the tutors. She has never been told "say it back to me" by someone who wanted to hear it. Her father pays, and nobody has ever asked what she thinks.
- **Her arc, tier by tier.**
  1. Tier 1: arrives by carriage with the tutors, smug, wins.
  2. Tier 2: still ahead, rattled that the player has no list: "How do you do that without a list?"
  3. Tier 3: her first stumble in Three Lamps Out. She blames the lamp. Later the player sees her alone behind the last lamp on the Quay, wiping her eyes. "Tell anyone and I shall have you fined."
  4. Tier 4: she sends the tutors home. She turns up with a plain notebook and asks, without looking at the player, how to choose what a thing should look like. "Do not read anything into it."
  5. Final: she walks her own route, the stairs of the nursery in Gilt House, the only place she has ever been alone. She is good. Win or lose, she shakes hands, and asks to practise together.
- **What she becomes.** She stays proud and funny. She is not reformed into a different person. She just gets more human: she can say "well done" and mean it. Never cruel, never humiliated, always beatable.

## Does the rival connect to the antagonist? Recommendation

**Recommended: the Cup, not the person.** The rival is not a secret villain and is not working for anyone. A hidden-villain twist would make her arc feel like a trick, and the owner wants her to become more human, not more evil.

The only connection is the Lamp Cup itself. The Games were Ambrose Tallow's invention: he set the Say-Back in order to keep the Vigil awake, and put the first Cup's lamp on its stand. His name wore off the founder's plate. The Gilt family have polished that blank plate for generations. In the ending (document 1, beat 7) the plate reads again, and Ottoline reads it aloud for the first time. She and the Winder are the same wound at two ages: both afraid of being nothing, both held up by someone else's keeping. The story shows it without saying it.

Alternatives, if the owner prefers:

- **A.** No connection at all. Simplest, and fine.
- **B.** The tutors were paid by the same hidden Patron as the Order. Gives a clue but turns her family into dupes.
- **C.** Ottoline is Tallow's descendant. Too neat, and it would make her arc about a bloodline instead of a choice.
