# Syllabus plan 1.3: two learners, one year (proposal, Nov 8, 2026)

Status: **built as 1.2.1 to 1.8.0 (Nov 8, 2026), owner decisions in section 7 taken as recommended. What is left is listed in docs/HANDOFF_SYLLABUS_PLAN.md.** It turns the champion audit (`docs/SYLLABUS_AUDIT.md`) into a plan, ties each piece to the story and lore of Veyra, and lists the decisions only the owner can make. The current syllabus as it ships is in `docs/SYLLABUS.md` (generated by `scripts/syllabus-doc.mjs`).

It assumes the 1.2 work in progress (Real-life Scenes, Carry it, languages, formulae, dates, jokes, diagrams) ships first. This plan is numbered 1.3 and later.

---

## 1. The answer in short

**Does the game suit the practical learner (Learner B)?** Mostly yes for *learning* the methods; **no for *keeping* their own material for months and years**. The methods for lists, names, numbers, talks, poems, words, formulae and dates are taught and drilled well, and the 1.2 scenes make them feel like real life. But there is nowhere to put your own vocabulary, formulae, dates, jokes or book notes and have them asked again for months; practice is never asked again after 30 days, own texts stop at 180 days, and nothing checks *understanding* (explaining a concept, using a formula, the argument of a book).

**Does the game suit the aspiring champion (Learner A)?** **No, and it was designed that way** (the dossier makes PAO and cards optional and weights speed at 10%). It is a good first three months for a future competitor, then it stops growing: four of the ten championship events are missing (binary, spoken numbers, abstract images, invented dates), there is no long-format training, no 3-digit or PAO system, no saved card system, and no plan for the hundreds of palace stops a competitor needs. As built, the year ends at about club level in words and names and below it in numbers and cards (reviewer's estimate, not checked).

**What changes the picture:**
1. **The Keeper's Ledger** (your own cards, reviewed forever) and **longer review gaps** fix Learner B's biggest hole for almost every topic at once.
2. **The Gloss** (explain it in your own words, then check it a day later) adds understanding to concepts, formulae and books.
3. **The Wardens' Road**, an optional Champion Track that opens at the Month 5 gate, gives Learner A every championship event, the bigger systems and a journey manager, while the core year stays a 30-minute practical course.

Honest ceiling with this plan (estimates, not checked): Learner B keeps chosen material for years with about 10 minutes of review a day. Learner A reaches the lower end of national level in two or three events after a year on the Wardens' Road; world level takes years of longer daily practice, and the game will say so.

## 3. For the practical learner (Learner B): the new pieces

Each piece names: what it is, how it is tested, the review schedule, where it lives in the story, and its size (S under a day, M one to three days, L a week or more).

### 3.1 The Keeper's Ledger (critical, size M to L)

**What.** Your own cue-and-answer cards in named books: "Spanish verbs", "Finance formulae", "My jokes", "History dates", "Book: Atomic Habits, chapter ideas". Each card has a front and a back and an optional picture note (your image, never required). A book can be tied to a palace so card 7 sits on stop 7. Optional reverse direction per book. Starter books from the game's checked pools (capitals, elements, formulae, word roots) for people who want to try it first.

**Tested.** Typed answer with the forgiving matcher, or "say it and mark yourself" for long answers. A miss shows the answer and sends the card back to day 1.

**Schedule.** 1, 3, 7, 14, 30, 60, 120, 240 days, then once a year forever. Daily caps (default 10 new cards, 40 reviews) keep it inside the 30 minutes. From week 5 the day opens with up to 5 minutes of Ledger reviews when any are due.

**Rewards.** A held review after 7 days or more earns a little Lumen inside the existing daily cap (same rule as Your Own Text). It never changes skill estimates. A card is "kept" at its first held review after 30 days.

**Covers:** your own vocabulary list (promised in M6 and never built), formulae, dates, jokes, book ideas, exam terms, presentation facts, recipe quantities.

**In the story.** On the night of the great Tide every ledger in the Archive came back blank. At the Month 1 gate Calder gives you one of those blank ledgers and says: "Paper forgets. That is what we learned. So write in it, if you like, but keep it the old way: the book only tells you what to ask yourself." Each book you start gets a spine on a shelf in your Long Gallery palace; a spine glows warmer as more of its cards pass the 30-day mark. Later, when you learn of the Heartledger (Month 12), Wren notices that your Ledger works the same way: "It is not the paper. It is you walking it." The Tide imagery stays gentle: a missed card is a Murmur or a Dustleaf that drifted, not a failure.

### 3.2 Longer review gaps for practice (critical, size S)

**What.** Practice sets with an immediate score of 80% or more are asked again at 1, 7, 30 and 90 days (today they stop at 30). A pass at 30 days or later needs 70% (today a low starter can pass at 50%). Gate sets keep 1, 7, 30, 90, 180. Only new sets change; old saved sets keep their own schedule.

**In the story.** 90-day items come back in the Long Vigil as **Old Echoes**, older and fainter Forgettings that climbed up from the Undercroft. Holding one lights a lamp on the battlements that stays lit for the season.

### 3.3 The Evening Round: keeping long texts for good (major, size M)

**What.** After its 180-day check, each section of Your Own Text joins a fixed rotation: the whole text is split into daily portions so every section is said at least once every 7, 14 or 30 days (your choice), forever. A slipped section drops back to the 1, 7, 30 ladder. This is how people keep long memorised texts: verses, scripture, poems, a speech, a part in a play. Raise the per-text size limit only after a phone performance check. No religious or treasured text ships with the game (owner decision stands); the player brings their own, and the screen reminds them to check it against a trusted printed copy.

**In the story.** The Lamplighter's Song is sung on the Quay every dusk, and nobody on the Quay has forgotten a verse in nine hundred years. Tansy explains why: "You do not learn a song once, dear. You sing a little of it every night, round and round, and it never has time to slip." The rotation is called the Evening Round, and the section due tonight appears as the lamplighters' verse of the evening.

### 3.4 The Gloss: explain it in your own words (major, size M)

**What.** After a Fact Recall, Mind Map, formula or Skim drill on real material, one prompt: "Explain why, or how, in one or two sentences, without looking." Then the model explanation is shown and you mark Kept, Partly or Missed. The next day the same prompt comes back, delayed. Self-marks earn nothing and never change skill estimates (self-report cannot be checked); the delayed result is shown only to you.

**Why.** Pictures store labels; understanding needs explaining and examples (Dunlosky and colleagues 2013 rate self-explanation and elaborative questioning as moderately useful, and imagery for text as low). This is the honest bridge between "I remember the word" and "I understand the idea".

**Content.** About 100 short model explanations (maps, formulae, skim texts, the 26 surprising facts already have a "why").

**In the story.** In Chapter 7 the scribes' two chronicles disagree. Brother Ennick teaches that a scribe who truly knows a passage can write a **gloss** in the margin, its meaning in plain words, and that the false chronicle was the one whose glosses did not match its text. Writing a gloss becomes a Scriptorium habit; Ennick keeps a row of your glosses on his desk ("Fewer mistakes than last month. I am writing that down too.").

### 3.5 Formulae done properly, with a finance set (major, size M plus checked content)

**What, in four steps:**
1. **The Merchants' Marks:** a fixed picture alphabet of about 35 symbols (=, +, minus, ×, ÷, fraction bar, square, cube, root, power, Σ, Δ, π, e, ln, log, %, sub and superscript, common Greek letters), drilled like the Major list.
2. **Whole formula:** recall the whole right-hand side, not one missing piece (the scorer already exists).
3. **One structure question** per formula that needs understanding ("If r doubles, the area...", "Which value goes on top in CAGR?").
4. **Use it once:** a small worked number per formula, checked like Mental Reckoning.

**Finance set (new, about 15 entries, each with a legend and a worked number, second checker required):** simple interest; compound interest with n periods a year; continuous compounding; CAGR = (end ÷ start)^(1 ÷ years) − 1; NPV = Σ CFₜ ÷ (1 + r)ᵗ − initial outlay; present and future value of an annuity; ROI; gross and net margin; P/E; current ratio; debt to equity; break-even units. Real, standard formulae only, with a line that this is memory practice, not financial advice.

**In the story.** The Counting House (Month 4) is where the Salt Guild's merchants worked sums aloud at the Reckoning Table. Their marks for "more", "less", "share" and "grows by" are the Merchants' Marks; Isolde teaches them, and the finance set lives in the Ledger of Debts from Chapter 4, the book Calder's entries stopped in mid-sum. Finishing the set lets you finish Calder's sum.

### 3.6 Language beyond nouns (major, size M per language)

**What.** For each language, 10 to 20 common irregular verbs with a "person plus tense to form" drill (yo, tener, present: tengo); pictures only for irregular stems, patterns taught as rules. Gender for more languages (French two, German three genders: fire, ice and a third element such as stone). An honest line in Month 6: "This month teaches you to keep words. Speaking a language takes listening, grammar and conversation as well." Optional audio for pronunciation on the review screen only, after recall (needs the owner's decision, section 7). Native-speaker review for every list.

**In the story.** The Tongues Bridge (Month 6) has the gargoyles that speak the old tongue. Verbs become the **turning stones** of the bridge: each stone turns to show a different face (I, you, we) and the bridge only lets you over when you say the right face. Gender elements match the bridge's two (later three) lanterns.

### 3.7 English vocabulary (major, size S on top of 3.1)

A "Words of the Archive" starter book for English: word to definition, definition to word, and one sentence of use, built on the word-roots set already shipped. It lives in the Ledger, so it gets the forever schedule.

### 3.8 Jokes, talks, recipes and books (small pieces)

- **The Joke Box (S on top of 3.1):** a Ledger book type where the theme is the room and the key word is the answer, plus a "tell it aloud" self-check. *Story:* Tansy's tea room has a box of jokes the Quay folk swap on Vigil nights to stay awake (the Say-Back began on the Long Vigil, `events.js`), and the box is sorted by topic like a little palace.
- **Talk rehearsal (S):** after the Week 28 outline drill, a "walk your outline aloud" timer, self-marked, no reward. *Story:* the Amphitheatre of Echoes, where speeches were once given from memory.
- **Recipes (S):** the recipe scene ties each quantity and oven temperature to its step image. *Story:* Tansy's rosemary tea and the Market food stalls.
- **Books (M):** Skim, Pick, Keep adds the Gloss for each kept idea, and kept ideas go into a Ledger book reviewed to 90 days and beyond. *Story:* travellers on the Open Roads (Chapter 10) carry books from outside Veyra; the **Travellers' Satchel** holds what you keep from them.

### 3.9 Dates and history beyond the year

Real events keep their checked sets. Add ordering questions (which came first) and reverse questions (what happened in 1848?), and let players build their own timeline as a Ledger book tied to a palace. *Story:* the Hall of Years and the Brass Years (the Tide rubbed the year labels off the frames; a scholar who knows the events can put them back).

### 3.10 Personal projects for Month 10

The Month 10 objective says "apply chosen techniques to 3 personal projects", but there is no record of a project. Add a simple project card (what, which method, a Ledger book or Own Text tied to it) with the same "only a held delayed check counts" rule. *Story:* each project is a road you travel on the Open Roads map; finishing three is how you "find out who you were" in the Month 10 quest.

## 5. For the aspiring champion (Learner A): the Wardens' Road

An **optional Champion Track** that opens at the Month 5 gate. On days you choose it, it replaces the scene and top-up rounds (so the day stays about 30 minutes), plus one longer **Long Sunday** a week (a 15-minute event and its recall). Results never change core skill estimates (same rule as the Crypt and the Event Hall); it keeps its own personal-best ladders. The game says plainly: "The Wardens' Road is a hobby path for people who want to try memory sport. Competitive levels usually take years of practice."

**In the story.** Veyra once had thirteen Lamp-Wardens, and "a Warden at the height of the Art could walk through a Tide as thick as wool". In the Month 5 chapter Isolde (or Calder) mentions that the Wardens did not stop at twelve routes; each Warden kept a discipline, and their tablets are scattered in the Undercroft and the sealed vaults. Walking the Wardens' Road means learning each lost Warden's discipline from their tablet. Ottoline Gilt, your rival from the Say-Back Games, is on the same road and her marks keep following your own earlier best. Finishing it earns the title **Warden of the Road**, a cosmetic, never a claim about real-world ability.

## 6. Bigger practice pools (both learners)

Small pools turn recall into recognition: 420 concrete words, 110 first names, 63 dated events. Grow the word pool to at least 2,000 (concrete, plus abstract nouns and verbs for the strict words format), names to 1,000 first names and 1,000 surnames (public, common names from many cultures, never private people), and use invented events for the competition dates format. Learner B content (formulae, real dates, facts) can stay small, because learning those for good is the point. The local Qwen helper can draft pools; every list is filtered and checked by hand before it ships (`CLAUDE.md`).

## 7. Decisions for the owner

1. **Wardens' Road (Champion Track):** build it, or keep the game a practical course only? It is the largest part of this plan (about four to six week-sized pieces). Recommended: yes, in blocks, after the Ledger.
2. **Spoken Numbers and audio.** `CLAUDE.md` allows no sound on study and recall screens except one soft submit sound. Spoken Numbers needs digits read aloud. Proposed exception: only inside the Wardens' Road, off by default, labelled as an audio event, using small pre-recorded digit files or the device's own voice (no network).
3. **Pronunciation audio for languages:** allow it on the review screen after recall only? Otherwise keep the say-it spellings.
4. **Ledger size limits** (proposed 50 books, 5,000 cards, 400 characters a side) and the Own Text size limit for long works. Both affect backup size and the 5 MB import cap.
5. **Names for the new pieces:** the Keeper's Ledger, Old Echoes, the Evening Round, the Gloss, the Merchants' Marks, turning stones, the Joke Box, the Travellers' Satchel, the Wardens' Road, the Players, Ennick's Book of Slips, Tallow's Hours. Keep, rename or drop any.
6. **New canon** this plan adds: the Wardens each kept a discipline and left tablets for it; the Quay lamps once signalled in rows of three; the Clockwork Market's hundred Players. None of it contradicts the current lore pages, but it is new canon and the lore guide would be rebuilt.
