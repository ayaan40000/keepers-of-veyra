# Design review input 2 (owner, Nov 2, 2026): four "high-concept" ideas

Four ideas from outside agents, each borrowing from a famous game. The owner wants them reviewed by the expert agents (memory champions, evidence reviewer, game designer) and the development plan updated. They may conflict with the project's hard rules (CLAUDE.md): rewards only from demonstrated learning, no time-spent rewards, no punishment or shame, no network or accounts, honest claims (no medical or memory-improvement claims), invented names only, study and recall screens still and quiet, ids permanent, Crypt and battle results never change skill estimates. Keep what fits, adapt what almost fits, refuse what does not, and say why.

## 1. The Cabinet of Voices (inspired by Disco Elysium)

Memory techniques are living personas in the Keeper's mind (the Method of Loci, the Major System, Interactive Imagery) who interrupt dialogue and gameplay with comments. When inspecting a scene or talking to an untrustworthy merchant, the skills chime in according to their level. A high Loci skill whispers structural secrets about the room ("this layout matches a house you lived in; use the fireplace as a locus for what he says next"). Examples of voices: Loci ("place the clue on the nightstand"), Major ("the code is 4321, an Army of Doves carrying a Big Clock"), Imagery ("his crooked jaw, picture a broken iron hinge"). If you fail a memory check in a daily session, the matching voice "suffers a psychological breakdown or gets choked out by the Lethe Tide", changing how that skill speaks to you until you patch the memory.

## 2. The Heartledger Echo (inspired by Outer Wilds)

All of Veyra is open from day one, with no artificial gates. The only key to any lock is what you can remember word for word from hidden historical tablets. You find an ancient code or blueprint early; the app does not save it in a quest log; you must encode it with a palace or keyword method. Days or weeks later at the matching sealed gate you type the sequence or vocabulary from memory to pass. If you forget, the path stays closed and you hunt for clues elsewhere. "The save file is in your actual head."

## 3. The Altered Reliquary (inspired by Alan Wake and Control)

The 76 relics manifest as pristine pixel-art objects on pedestals in the walkable palace room when immediate and delayed recall probes pass. If a 7-day or 30-day probe slips, that relic decays and bleeds shadow into the room, "turning a safe haven into a survival horror environment" with shadow creatures. To banish the darkness you complete a "high-stress" Recall Card challenge to fix the weak imagery.

## 4. The Social Link with Your Own Past (inspired by Persona)

The player is described as "a Sales Manager in Texas trying to build a new life while navigating an ancient mystery". Daily interactions with Wren and the citizens are a high-stakes social calendar with limited daily "Mental Focus" time slots to spend on drilling, exploring or talking to Wren. A deep bond with Wren requires remembering her personal details, fears and family history across weeks (names, faces, narrative structure). Remembering a detail she told you three weeks ago in a crucial dialogue choice ranks up her "Social Link", which modifies the adaptive learning engine to grant bonus Focus XP or lowers time pressure on timed Crypt runs.
