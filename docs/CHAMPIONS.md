# Memory Champion panel: findings (Nov 1, 2026)

Three reviewers read the game from the lens of published champion methods, practical coaching and memory science. **None could search the web; every fact is from memory.** Individual records, years and training volumes are the least certain. Do not name an athlete or quote a figure in the game until it is checked. The Masters' Notes therefore describe methods in general and name nobody. Dresler et al. 2017 (Neuron: about six weeks of 30 minutes a day of palace practice raised recall of the trained kind of list and changed brain connectivity) is the one study the game cites, always with "this does not show a better memory for everything".

## Web fact-check (Nov 1, 2026): corrections that override everything below

A reviewer with web search checked the claims. Most athlete-to-method links were **not** supported, so the game names nobody against a method.

- **Dresler et al. 2017 (Neuron):** 51 men, no mnemonic experience, method of loci vs an n-back control vs no training; **40 sessions of 30 minutes over six weeks** (not 40 days in a row); word-list recall rose from about 26 to 62 of 72 and stayed high four months later; connectivity moved toward the pattern seen in 23 memory athletes. It does not show better memory for other material, any effect in women or older people, or any effect on IQ or daily life. Boris Konrad is Dresler's co-researcher (author list not fully confirmed).
- **Maguire et al. 2003:** 10 memory-championship competitors vs 10 matched controls, no exceptional IQ or structural brain differences, 9 of 10 used the method of loci. Say "no exceptional intellectual ability", not "normal IQ".
- **SF (Chase and Ericsson):** about 80 digits after more than 200 hours; popular figure 7 to 79 digits in about 230 hours. Say "about 80 digits after more than 200 hours".
- **Not supported, do not attribute in the game:** error review to Alex Mullen; any named method to Ben Pridmore, Yanjaa Wintersoul, Simon Reinhard, Johannes Mallow or Jonas von Essen; "personal best ladder", "format day" or "sprint round" to anyone.
- **Safe factual credits (public achievements only, never an endorsement):** Dominic O'Brien (eight-time World Memory Champion, Dominic System, 2005 book); Alex Mullen (champion 2015 to 2017, three-digit Major System images, says he trains under an hour a day, often about 30 minutes); Ben Pridmore (champion 2004, 2008, 2009); Jonas von Essen (champion 2013 and 2014); Nelson Dellis (record holder for USA titles, author of Remember It!); Boris Konrad (neuroscientist and memory athlete); Simon Reinhard (set world records); Joshua Foer (author, 2006 USA champion, confirm first). Wait to name Mallow, Wintersoul and Cooke.
- **Scoring:** the world championships have ten events (cards, numbers, words, faces and others); Memory League has six head-to-head disciplines. Do not put point scores or timings in the game.
- **Science:** Melby-Lervag and Hulme 2013 and the 2016 update confirmed: working-memory training helps similar tasks, with no good evidence of transfer. Dunlosky et al. 2013 rates practice testing and spacing high and the keyword method low (short retention, limited material), so the game must not call keywords the best long-term way to keep words.
- **Never say:** "40 days in a row"; "26 to 62" without "this one kind of list"; exact title counts other than the ones above; any athlete endorses Mnemos; general memory, IQ or decline protection.

## Fact-check of the owner's real-life notes (`FIELD_GUIDE_SOURCES.md`)

- The game teaches the **Major System** (M4) and PAO (M9), not the letter-based Dominic System. The Field Guide uses Major only. The source also had the Dominic code wrong (real: 1=A 2=B 3=C 4=D 5=E 6=S 7=G 8=H 9=N 0=O; the source said 6=F), "Boba" Fett, and the sharps phrase covered only F C G.
- **PINs and passwords: cut.** No advice on memorising passwords, PINs, bank or door codes, or medicine times.
- **Speed math:** pictures usually cost more time than holding a four-digit result. The guide says to calculate left to right and say partial results aloud, and keeps pictures for constants and formulas.
- Physical skills: a palace stores the order of steps only; movement needs practice and a coach. Coding: syntax comes from writing code. Books: a picture is a label, not the argument; say the idea in your own words first.

## What the evidence supports (safe sentences)

"Pictures and places help you remember lists, names and sequences when you use them on purpose." "It works best on things you must recall in order or exactly." "Checking yourself later is what makes it last." Never: general memory or IQ gains, protection from decline, exam or career guarantees, "learn a book in a day". Mnemos mnemonics store arbitrary items; they do not build understanding.

## Battle design advice (all three reviewers agree)

- **Enemy = a "Forgetting" that holds a set of items from an earlier module.** Its HP is the items still lost; type matches the skill (Imagery, Link, Palace, Face, Major, Vocab, Fact). Player HP is **Resolve** (about 5). A turn is one recall prompt. A correct recall is the attack, with fixed damage scaled by item difficulty, never by speed alone. A wrong answer costs one Resolve and the enemy keeps the item. Losing ends gently: answers are shown and the items queue for a quiet review. No loss of progress, no streak penalty.
- **Moves are question formats, not power-ups:** Recall (free entry), Order, Pair (cue to response), Reverse, Locate (palace stop to item). A Hint reveals the first cue and halves that answer's damage.
- **No multiple choice with a fast timer** (guessing). Free entry or cued typing, with the existing forgiving matcher; show the stored answer after any miss. No negative marking. No timer on recall (speed only above 80% accuracy, as the dossier already scores).
- **Deterministic damage.** No random loot, critical hits or variable rewards (slot-machine schedule, refused). No daily quota, no energy timer, no FOMO or limited-time events.
- **Re-tests are pulled by spacing, not purely at random:** items due at 1, 7, 30, 90 days from the existing probe sets; randomness chooses which due items and the order. Interleave modules in one battle. Mostly items previously held, a few earlier misses, never brand-new material. Show why an item appeared ("learned on day 41, last held on day 71"). After a miss, show the answer and re-ask later in the same battle (two retries per item at most).
- **Telegraph ambushes:** "A Memory Echo stirs" before the room. At most one per run, fewer for new players, skippable once, no penalty.
- **Battle results never change skill estimates** (same as the Crypt). Rewards only from old items that held: story, cosmetics, small capped Lumen. Winning on easy fresh items earns nothing.
- **Technique choice (M10):** show the material, a coach asks "what is this made of?", then a menu of 3 or 4 moves (Link, Palace, Major, Keyword). A poor fit gives a small tip and a harder recall, not a penalty; a good fit gives more study time.
- **Real-life scenes with invented, non-sensitive material:** Networking Gala (names and faces, "ask again" once per guest), Packing Bay (a list with a mid-way distractor), Speech Hall (five points along a route), Crossroads (six turns), Night Market (prices), Recipe stall, Library (four key ideas).
- **Items support, never replace, memory:** a Scroll of Order shows how many items (not what), a Lantern shows the first letter after you commit an answer, a Palace Map shows empty stops. Nothing shows an answer before recall. Companions are coaches who speak before and after, never during recall.
- Battle length 5 to 8 minutes, can be left. Study and recall screens stay still and quiet; battle flair goes between turns and on the result screen. Honest line for the player: "Battles practise the methods on made-up material. They are not a test of real-life memory, and they do not make memory better in general."

## Outside retention advice: verdicts

- **Keep:** learning as the magic system; skills shown as stats; a story over streaks; customisation and lore and side quests; difficulty that follows accuracy; a finite daily session; forgiving streaks; a visual palace blueprint (already there as the walkable palace).
- **Refuse:** variable-ratio rewards, streak FOMO, daily login rewards, limited-time events, sunk-cost hooks, energy timers, leaderboards. Bonus "Focus XP" for returning after absence (rewards coming back, not learning): catch-up is a lighter review instead.
- **Unproven, do not claim:** the Zeigarnik effect pulling people back (replication is poor); "people rarely abandon a story".
- **Guilds and a shared 10,000 HP raid boss: defer.** Mnemos has no accounts or network. A team HP bar fed by cards reviewed rewards volume and creates shame pressure. Safe local version: NPC companions in the story; if ever a shared boss, no deadline, damage only from delayed holds, never show who gave less.
- "Brain fog" must not be a medical claim. Offer an "I am tired today" lighter session. The 10 to 15 minute cap is not evidence-based; Mnemos stays at about 30 minutes.
- **Reject from the outside curriculum:** Dominic System and number-shape pegs for "pins and lock codes"; a four-season rewrite of the year (the existing 12-module curriculum stays). The weekly boss ideas (Supermarket Sweep, Networking Gala, Archive Siege, World Memory Championship) become battle scenes.
