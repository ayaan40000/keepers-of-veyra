# Design review input (owner, Nov 1, 2026)

Two outside pieces of advice the owner wants reviewed by the game designer and the memory champions. They come from reviewers who do not know this game. Several points conflict with the project's hard rules (see CLAUDE.md): the review must keep what fits and reject what does not, saying why.

## Part 1: general retention advice for an educational RPG

**Why games hook people (as the advice describes it):**
1. Compulsion loop: action, reward, expansion (fight, get gold, buy a better sword). Variable rewards such as random loot release more dopamine.
2. Zeigarnik effect: a quest log of half-finished tasks creates "cognitive tension" that pulls you back.
3. Visible progression metrics and the Progress Principle: XP bars, levels, skill trees show competence.
4. Loss aversion and sunk cost: daily login rewards, limited-time events, long-term investment, streak FOMO.

**Blueprint for sustainable retention:**
1. Learning as the power source. Learning is the magic system, not a tax. Correct answers cast spells; stats map to subjects; skill nodes unlock from demonstrated learning.
2. Epic meaning and a narrative arc instead of transactional streaks. People abandon a streak, but rarely a story halfway through.
3. Intrinsic over extrinsic progression: personal customisation (a wizard tower, avatar armour), new modes, hidden lore and side quests, dynamically scaled difficulty (flow), instead of static badges, leaderboards or energy timers.
4. Finite daily loops against burnout: rest mechanics, a 15 to 20 minute daily story quest, diminishing gains after it.
5. Social cohesion: guilds instead of leaderboards. A study guild fights a raid boss with 10,000 HP; every flashcard the team reviews deals damage. Social accountability beats competitive stress.

## Part 2: an independent reviewer's "Mind Palace Academy" (for busy adults 30+)

Memory as a character class ("The Mental Cartographer"), techniques as unlocked abilities; users build, expand and defend a kingdom. Sessions capped at 10 to 15 minutes; a spaced-repetition recovery system like mana regeneration.

Four seasons: Q1 Foundations of Imagery (link and story, number-shape pegs, first palace; weekly boss "Supermarket Sweep": a random 20-item list through the palace under a timer). Q2 Encoding Systems (Dominic System people, actions, PAO; boss "The Networking Gala": 10 NPCs with names, faces, 10-digit phone numbers, birthdays). Q3 Infinite Realm Architecture (external palaces, virtual and fiction palaces, palace maintenance with spaced repetition; boss "The Archive Siege": rapid trivia and timelines to allocate to specialised palaces). Q4 Grandmaster Applications (verbatim text and language, speaking without notes with a 15-station route, card memorisation; final raid "The World Memory Championship": a deck of cards under 2 minutes, 100 digits, 50 dates, 30 names and faces).

Retention mechanics: a visual blueprint map where users drop palace nodes; professional co-op guilds (med school, developers, speakers) building shared reference palaces; "brain fog" guard rails: a missed few days never wipes a streak, the character rests at an inn and earns bonus Focus XP for catching up.
